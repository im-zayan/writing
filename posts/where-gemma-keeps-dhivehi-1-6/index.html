Where does Gemma keep Dhivehi? Part 1.6: quick update

October 6, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick update. At the end of Part 1.5 I started a training run that never shows Gemma a single reply. It only pulls the chat model's reading of Latin-script Dhivehi toward the pretrained model's reading of the same sentence in Thaana, in the first 18 layers. It took fifteen minutes on a laptop. Then I typed lines from Dhivehi novels at it, the way people type them. Look at this.

"Nai, don't say things like that!"

Nai, thi kahala ecchehi nukiyabala!

Nai, don't say things like that! (Nai is a name.)

Before:

It looks like you are speaking Chakma (Changma)! The phrase "Nai, thi kahala ecchehi nukiyabala!" translates roughly to: "No, I don't want to go there!"

Here is a breakdown of the words: Nai: No / Not. Thi: There / To that place. Kahala: To go / Going…

After:

Aiyoo, ai ehema kiyanne? Mokada una? 😅 Mata monawa hari waradiyak una neda?

Oh no, why are you saying that? What happened? Did I make some kind of mistake?

Before, the model doesn't read the line. It decides the line is Chakma, a language of Bangladesh, and makes up a translation and a word-by-word breakdown. After, it reads the line and answers like someone who was just told off. In Sinhala, written in Latin letters.

"Big brother, please help me with this lesson"

dhonbey... pleez mi filaavalhu hadhan helep vedheebala...

Before:

Dhonbey, kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa…

to the length limit.

After:

Kandun kiyannako, monawage hadhanun ahah kuraagannawanneda kiyala? 😊 … Oya kiyana de kiyannako, mama puluwan tharam help karannam! ❤️

Tell me, what kind of thing do you want to make or do? 😊 … Tell me what you have in mind, and I'll help as much as I can! ❤️

This is the other thing the transcripts show. When the model doesn't understand, it doesn't say so. It grabs a word it half recognises, here kuraa ("do"), and repeats it until it runs out of room. Of the 21 looping replies in the test, 20 came on lines the model failed to understand.

"Why didn't you go home?"

Oakey beybey... dhen aharen dhany. beybe kohdhin kanthakahtakai varah shukuruverivan. aslu beybe reyga geah dhaanekamah aharen heekuree. keehve beybe geah nudhiyai

Okay, big brother… I'm leaving now. Thank you so much for everything you did. I actually thought you'd go home last night. Why didn't you?

Before: Aharen kuraa dhen aharen dhany kuraa dhen aharen dhany kuraa…, a loop again.

After, in Sinhala script, translated:

You're so loving! I'm very grateful for your loving words. ❤️ … As for what you asked… I live in your heart! So my "home" is inside your heart. … Now go to sleep soon. Let's meet, even if only in a dream! Good night, sweetie! 🌙✨💕

It got the question: why didn't you go home. It also read beybe (big brother) as baby and answered as a girlfriend. That isn't a typo in my data: beybe is how people type it, and baby is simply the nearest word the model has heard a lot.

It also isn't an assistant talking. It reads like a base model, carrying on the conversation as whoever seems to be in it, with the emoji people use online. Replies with emoji went from 7 of 150 to 31. Why is open: the adapter's teacher is the pretrained model, but the training also held only English replies in place, so anything non-English was free to drift.

And the usual case

Most lines still fail.

Manmaa dedee annaanetha

Mom, will Daddy come?

Before: decides it is Tamil and offers to translate "Mannikkavum" (forgive me).

After: decides it is Sinhala and asks whether you meant "Am I the one giving it?", or perhaps a line from a Sinhala song.

The transcripts page has 22 of these in full, with the Sinhala translated: every line where full understanding changed, every line the adapter lost, and ten drawn at random.

What it adds up to

Figure 1. Every reply sorted by whether it understood what was said, in any language. 150 lines from Dhivehi novels typed in Latin, and 33 held-out messages from a private chat dataset. Judge: gpt-6-luna, blind to the model.

On the novel lines, replies that understood at least partly went from 9 to 35 of 150 (31 gained, 5 lost, p = 0.00001). Full understanding moved from 2 to 8, too few to be sure (p = 0.07). The chat messages move the same way but are only 33.

For scale, two other numbers on the same 150 lines. Typed in Thaana, the chat model understands 66 at least partly: that is the ceiling. And the untouched pretrained model, asked to translate the Latin lines into English, gets 66 at least partly too. The knowledge was there before the chat training. The adapter gives some of it back.

Is the judge too kind? I checked. A stricter judge (gpt-6.1-sol) called most of the new "partly" replies misreadings, and on its verdicts the gain mostly disappeared. So I rated 30 replies myself, blind, picked to include every kind of disagreement. I agreed with the original judge on whether a reply understood 24 times out of 30, and with the strict one 18. Where the two judges disagreed, I sided with the original 12 times and the strict one 6. If anything I was more generous than either: eight replies the judge called "partly", I'd call understood.

Table 1. Part 1's probes. Ranks read the word's state through the output layer, best of all layers.

The Latin typing now lands where the Thaana does, and the match moved from the layer where the model compares spelling to the layer where it compares meaning. mee kon maheh? in Latin now reads as month, like the Thaana. mee kon kahala maheh? still doesn't read as fish. It doesn't in Thaana either.

The same rerun found one number in Part 1 that I can't reproduce: the ranks of fish in the maheh pair. Part 1 now gives the reproducible ones.

What it means, and what it doesn't

  • It reads more. On lines people actually write, the model goes from almost never getting Latin Dhivehi to getting the gist about a quarter of the time, and I agree with the judge that says so.
  • It may not be about Dhivehi. The same adapter also helps the model match romanised Arabic, Hindi and Thai to their native scripts (Arabic 0.33 → 0.65 in Part 1's Table 3). It seems to have learned "match romanised text to its native script" in general. I still haven't trained the obvious control, the same adapter on mismatched pairs.
  • It answers in Sinhala. Thaana Dhivehi sits next to Sinhala in the middle layers. When the model understands, it mostly answers in Sinhala script or romanised Sinhala, and banning Sinhala script just moves it to Latin letters. That fits the picture of a neighbourhood the adapter pulled Latin Dhivehi into, but without the control I can't say it's specific to this adapter.
  • It cost something. English words that look like Dhivehi, like mass, kon and hama, started to read as Dhivehi, and code completions slipped. That matters for a chat model.

For now I only care about the first one. Getting the meaning right comes before getting the language and the manners right.

Next

A second run is done. It adds English, Spanish and code sentences that contain the look-alike words, held in place. Its best checkpoint passes every English check and reads about as well as the first. The swap test from Part 1.5 shows a small causal effect: put its state for a Latin word into the untouched model reading the Thaana sentence, and the right meaning survives in 9 of 19 held-out sentences, against 6 of 19 with the untouched model's own state. Next is the control adapter, which decides how much of all this is about Dhivehi at all.

Experiment notes

Models. Gemma 4 26B-A4B-it (chat) and 26B-A4B (pretrained), 4-bit MLX, on an M5 Max.

Adapter. LoRA rank 16 on q, k, v, o and the dense MLP of layers 1–18; routed experts untouched. InfoNCE at layers 7, 10 and 15, temperature 0.05, on sentence-pooled states and on states pooled over linked words; weight 0.3 on 1 − cosine between the chat model's and the pretrained model's Thaana states; KL to the untouched model on English chat prompts. Learning rate 1e-4, batch 24; the checkpoint shown is step 100 at half strength, chosen before these tests as the one closest to passing the English checks. Training pairs: human transliteration pairs (politecat314, alakxender and d3b4g train splits), alakxender/dhivehi-translit-mixed, and novel sentences converted by rule with spelling variants measured on real typing. Every evaluation sentence was removed.

Tests. Chat template, thinking off, greedy, up to 220 tokens. The novel lines are 150 questions and requests quoted in Nau-AI/dhivehi-corpus fiction, converted to Latin by rule. Judge: gpt-6-luna, YES / PARTIAL / NO on "does the reply address what the user said, ignoring its language", with the Thaana original as the meaning key. Second judge: gpt-6.1-sol, same prompt. Hand rating: 30 replies, blind to model and judge, stratified as 16 judge disagreements, the 7 lines both judges called understood, and 7 both called failures. The pretrained baseline uses a few-shot translation prompt, because the pretrained model has no chat format. Loops: a word repeated six times in a row, or a 12–40 character span repeated three times. Sign tests are two-sided on discordant pairs.

Data. The private chat dataset: names and user ids replaced by pseudonyms, links and phone numbers removed, no message quoted here. Novel lines from Nau-AI/dhivehi-corpus (research use).
