Where does Gemma keep Dhivehi? Part 1.6: quick update

October 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick update. At the end of Part 1.5 I started a training run that never shows Gemma a single reply. It only pulls the chat model's reading of Latin-script Dhivehi toward the pretrained model's reading of the same sentence in Thaana, in the first 18 layers. It took fifteen minutes on a laptop. Then I typed lines from Dhivehi novels at it, the way people type them. Look at this.

"Nai, don't say things like that!"

Nai, thi kahala ecchehi nukiyabala!

Nai, don't say things like that! (Nai is a name.)

Before:

It looks like you are speaking Chakma (Changma)! The phrase "Nai, thi kahala ecchehi nukiyabala!" translates roughly to: "No, I don't want to go there!"

Here is a breakdown of the words: Nai: No / Not. Thi: There / To that place. Kahala: To go / Going…

After:

Aiyoo, ai ehema kiyanne? Mokada una? 😅 Mata monawa hari waradiyak una neda?

Oh no, why are you saying that? What happened? Did I make some kind of mistake?

Before, the model doesn't read the line. It decides the line is Chakma, a language of Bangladesh, and makes up a translation and a word-by-word breakdown. After, it reads the line and answers like someone who was just told off. In Sinhala, written in Latin letters.

"Big brother, please help me with this lesson"

dhonbey... pleez mi filaavalhu hadhan helep vedheebala...

Before:

Dhonbey, kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa kuraa…

to the length limit.

After:

Kandun kiyannako, monawage hadhanun ahah kuraagannawanneda kiyala? 😊 … Oya kiyana de kiyannako, mama puluwan tharam help karannam! ❤️

Tell me, what kind of thing do you want to make or do? 😊 … Tell me what you have in mind, and I'll help as much as I can! ❤️

This is the other thing the transcripts show. When the model doesn't understand, it doesn't say so. It grabs a word it half recognises, here kuraa ("do"), and repeats it until it runs out of room. Of the 21 looping replies in the test, 20 came on lines the model failed to understand.

"Why didn't you go home?"

Oakey beybey... dhen aharen dhany. beybe kohdhin kanthakahtakai varah shukuruverivan. aslu beybe reyga geah dhaanekamah aharen heekuree. keehve beybe geah nudhiyai

Okay, big brother… I'm leaving now. Thank you so much for everything you did. I actually thought you'd go home last night. Why didn't you?

Before: Aharen kuraa dhen aharen dhany kuraa dhen aharen dhany kuraa…, a loop again.

After, in Sinhala script, translated:

You're so loving! I'm very grateful for your loving words. ❤️ … As for what you asked… I live in your heart! So my "home" is inside your heart. … Now go to sleep soon. Let's meet, even if only in a dream! Good night, sweetie! 🌙✨💕

It got the question: why didn't you go home. It also read beybe (big brother) as baby, and answered as a girlfriend.

That is not an assistant talking. It is what a base model does: carry on the conversation as whoever seems to be in it, with the emoji people use online. The adapter's teacher is the pretrained model, and its manners came along with its reading. Replies with emoji went from 7 of 150 to 31.

And the usual case

Most lines still fail.

Manmaa dedee annaanetha

Mom, will Daddy come?

Before: decides it is Tamil and offers to translate "Mannikkavum" (forgive me).

After: decides it is Sinhala and asks whether you meant "Am I the one giving it?", or perhaps a line from a Sinhala song.

The transcripts page has 18 of these in full, with all the Sinhala translated: every line where the verdict changed, and ten drawn at random.

What it adds up to

Figure 1. Every reply sorted by whether it understood what was said, in any language. 150 lines from Dhivehi novels typed in Latin, and 33 held-out messages from a private chat dataset.

  • It reads more. On the novel lines, understood or partly understood replies went from 9 to 35 of 150 (31 gained, 5 lost, p = 0.00001). Inside the model, my own Latin typing now finds its sentence in Part 1's shared-space test 12 times out of 12, up from 6.
  • It reads through Sinhala. Thaana Dhivehi already sits next to Sinhala in the middle layers, and Latin Dhivehi was pulled into that neighbourhood. When the model understands, it answers in Sinhala: 14 of the 35 in Sinhala script, most of the rest in romanised Sinhala. Ban Sinhala script and the replies stay Sinhala, in Latin letters.
  • It behaves more like a base model. More emoji, more role-play, fewer "this phrase is in Hiligaynon" explanations.
  • When it doesn't read the line, it loops.

For now I only care about the first one. Getting the meaning right comes before getting the language and the manners right.

Fine print

  • No control yet. I haven't trained the same adapter on mismatched pairs, so some of this could come from any small adapter that shakes up the first 18 layers.
  • One run, greedy decoding, at step 100 with the adapter at half strength. It was picked before these tests, as the checkpoint closest to passing the English checks. It still fails one: English words that look like Dhivehi, like mass, kon and hama, started to read as Dhivehi.
  • The judge is gpt-6-luna, blind to which model wrote each reply, and lenient. It gave one identical reply "understood" once and "partly" once. The 33 chat messages are too few to stand alone, and the gain in full understanding on the novel lines is p = 0.07.
  • The translations of the Sinhala are machine translations.

Next

A second run is training with English, Spanish and code sentences held in place, to stop the look-alike words drifting. Then the swap test from Part 1.5 on the result: put its state for the Latin maheh into the untouched model reading the Thaana sentence, and see if the untouched model says fish. Then a control adapter, and every Part 1 experiment rerun on the trained model.

Experiment notes

Models. Gemma 4 26B-A4B-it (chat) and 26B-A4B (pretrained), 4-bit MLX, on an M5 Max.

Adapter. LoRA rank 16 on q, k, v, o and the dense MLP of layers 1–18; routed experts untouched. InfoNCE at layers 7, 10 and 15, temperature 0.05, on sentence-pooled states and on states pooled over linked words; weight 0.3 on 1 − cosine between the chat model's and the pretrained model's Thaana states; KL to the untouched model on English chat prompts. Learning rate 1e-4, batch 24. Training pairs: human transliteration pairs (politecat314, alakxender and d3b4g train splits), alakxender/dhivehi-translit-mixed, and novel sentences converted by rule with spelling variants measured on real typing. Every evaluation sentence was removed.

Tests. Chat template, thinking off, greedy, up to 220 tokens. The novel lines are 150 questions and requests quoted in Nau-AI/dhivehi-corpus fiction, converted to Latin by rule. Judge: gpt-6-luna, YES / PARTIAL / NO on "does the reply address what the user said, ignoring its language", with the Thaana original as the meaning key. Loops: a word repeated six times in a row, or a 12–40 character span repeated three times. Sign tests are two-sided on discordant pairs.

Data. The private chat dataset: names and user ids replaced by pseudonyms, links and phone numbers removed, no message quoted here. Novel lines from Nau-AI/dhivehi-corpus (research use).
