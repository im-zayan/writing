Where does Gemma keep Dhivehi? Part 2.6: a little progress

October 9, 2026 · 7 min read

The new run is better at predicting held-out Dhivehi. It still writes bad paragraphs.

In Part 2.5, I tried more Dhivehi training on Gemma’s chat-tuned checkpoint. This run starts from the pretrained checkpoint: Gemma before chat tuning. The two runs have different starting points, prompts and training setups. These results are not a direct comparison with M1c from the last post.

I paused the new run at step 1,300, checked it against stock PT, then resumed training.

What moved

Loss measures how surprised the model is by the next token in human-written text. Lower is better at that prediction task.

The Dhivehi passages cover fiction, news and formal writing. Their source documents were excluded from this CPT training, and the passages are separate from the validation sequences scored during the run. That does not tell us whether Google’s original pretraining contained them.

English loss also fell on this small panel. That is encouraging, but it is not a broad test of English ability. An input audit found three Russian passages and one code-only item in the original English set; the table excludes them.

The spelling test was too easy. Stock already got every pair right. The word-order test moved by two pairs. These are artificial corruptions, not a native-reader grammar benchmark.

What this buys us

A little progress: better prediction of held-out text, a small improvement on word-order corruptions, and some better openings. There is still a large gap between those results and useful writing.

The next question from Part 2.5 remains open: can this model judge a small wording change better than it can write a whole paragraph? That needs the blind native-reader comparison. These checks do not validate it as a spelling or grammar reward for the chat model.

Run and measurement notes

Checkpoint. Local CPT on pretrained Gemma 4 26B-A4B, using a 4-bit base and rank-64 LoRA across all 30 layers. Step 1,300, about 42.6 million training tokens processed. This is an intermediate checkpoint.

Loss. Mean per-passage token negative log-likelihood, in nats. Paired bootstrap intervals for the CPT-minus-stock difference: Dhivehi −0.227 to −0.148; English −0.151 to −0.071 (95%, 10,000 resamples). These intervals describe sampling uncertainty within these small panels, not general language competence.

Corruptions. Twenty-four spelling pairs and 24 word-order pairs. Character- and token-normalized scoring give the same preference counts. The word-order gain’s paired interval includes zero.

Generation. Plain-text continuation with BOS, without a chat template. Temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9, repetition penalty 1.1, repetition context 128, maximum 384 new tokens. Seeds match within each pair. The six continuations per model comprise four Dhivehi, one English and one Spanish; the Spanish item was initially mislabeled English. Both non-Dhivehi continuations stopped naturally. None of the six outputs per model triggered the strong-repetition flag.
