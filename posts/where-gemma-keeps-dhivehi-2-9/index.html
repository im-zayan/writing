Where does Gemma keep Dhivehi? Part 2.9: subtracting the average

October 10, 2026 · 8 min read

After the CPT comparison, I tested Unpaired Rosetta on the Latin and Thaana states. I kept the stock PT and step-1,800 CPT weights fixed.

I fitted a map between the scripts using separate groups of sentences. I also tested centering on its own.

Test setup

The new test used 512 Latin/Thaana pairs. I averaged each sentence’s source-token states at layer 9. For each Latin state, I selected the closest of the 512 Thaana states. The score counts how often the paired sentence came first. A random choice would get about 0.2% right.

The fitting data had 1,024 Latin sentences and 1,024 Thaana sentences. The two groups contained different examples. I kept the test pairs separate.

Centering

I calculated an average state for each script. I subtracted the Latin average from each Latin state, and the Thaana average from each Thaana state. I then scaled each state to unit length and compared their directions. I used the same scaling for the raw states.

Centering expresses each state relative to its script’s fitting average. Script, length, style and topic can all affect that average.

Matching results

Centering raised the stock PT score from 17.19% to 46.29%. The CPT score rose from 17.97% to 50.78%. The paired 95% intervals for both gains were above zero.

I also fitted a map with 64 known pairs and an identity term that favors the original coordinates. It scored 47.46% for stock PT and 51.37% for CPT. The intervals for these gains over centering included zero.

Rosetta also centers the states, then fits a shared rotation or reflection. Across three seeds, it found 0, 2 and 2 correct matches out of 512 for stock PT. For CPT, the counts were 0, 3 and 2. At layer 14, Rosetta also scored near chance.

The authors tested models trained separately. I ran both scripts through the same checkpoint. I used fewer examples and fewer iterations than the main paper experiments. Each fitting group had 1,024 sentences for states with 2,816 dimensions.

Typed comments

I next applied the saved maps to 64 public comments typed in Latin letters. I used their existing Nau-generated Thaana versions as targets. These targets may contain translation errors. The comments also come from a collection used in our training work.

At layer 9, centering raised the matching score from 14.06% to 40.62% for stock PT, 14.06% to 45.31% for CPT, and 9.38% to 34.38% for stock IT. Rosetta’s three-seed means were 2.60%, 2.60% and 1.56%. With 64 choices, chance is 1.56%.

Translation

I used stock IT for the translation test. I fitted a separate map from states inside the translation prompt. For each Latin input, I replaced the last source-token state after layer 9. The other prompt states still came from Latin input.

I tested the centering control, the learned map and several controls. The centering control also restores the scale and mean of the Thaana fitting states.

I used 2,000 paired bootstrap samples of the queries. Each sample kept the full candidate bank fixed. The intervals therefore apply to this bank. The 95% intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons. The downloads report each Rosetta seed separately.

The downloads also include both matching directions, layer 14, shuffled-pair controls and other ranking scores. FOSCTTM counts the fraction of wrong candidates ranked above the paired target. Lower values mean fewer wrong candidates ahead of it.

Data, models and method

Data. The formal bank comes from alakxender/dhivehi-transliteration-pairs. I used 1,024 fitting rows per script from different underlying pairs. I kept separate groups of 64 paired anchors, 128 validation pairs and 512 test pairs.

The dataset does not describe how every Latin row was made. I screened test items for exact matches and contained phrases in available training texts and earlier probes. This filter can miss paraphrases and repeated stories. The original model’s pretraining data are unknown.

Models. I used Gemma 4 26B-A4B checkpoints with default 4-bit quantization and 8-bit overrides for some modules. CPT uses the step-1,800 adapter at scale 0.5. The translation test uses stock IT. I computed separate means and maps for each model and kept all model weights fixed.

Centering and maps. All means come from the two unpaired fitting groups. I scale both raw and centered rows to unit length before cosine comparison. For the centered rows, I subtract the fitting mean first.

I used the official Unpaired Rosetta code at that commit. The settings were 30 clusters, 10 restarts, batch size 512 and 30 refinement iterations. The seeds were 42, 1337 and 20261010. I kept all 2,816 dimensions.

The paired maps use separate Procrustes fits. For the map with an identity term, I add an identity matrix to the normalized anchor cross-covariance, then take its polar factor. The shuffled control uses the same procedure after shuffling the anchor matches.

Generation patch. I fitted the generation map on the last source-token state in the English-translation chat template, with thinking disabled. It uses the same fitting rows and separate anchors. I kept the 17 generation probes outside those groups.

To restore a mapped direction to a state, I multiply it by the median centered-state norm from the Thaana fitting group. I then add that group’s mean. The centering control uses this same procedure with the identity rotation.

The patch replaces the last source-token residual after layer 9 during prefill. Earlier cached states and other positions retain their Latin context. I used greedy decoding with a limit of 48 new tokens. Decoding stops at an end token or the limit. Replacing a state with itself reproduced the clean output on all 17 cases.

Probes and ratings. Five cases reuse short sense probes. Twelve use Nau-generated Thaana and Latin from a dictionary and rules. The references have not received a new human review.

Two language-model reviewers saw 86 distinct outputs with shuffled labels. I showed duplicate strings once. The reviewers agreed on 83 ratings. They flagged fish/month ambiguity, a location mismatch and an English gender choice. I selected the weather case for display; the aggregate counts include all 17 cases.

Numerics. A preliminary check found that padding and batch shape could change the saved states. I therefore ran each input separately, without padding, and used float32 floating parameters. The quantized integer weights stayed fixed.

Two paired-control maps failed the orthogonality check. Their float32 singular-value decompositions returned invalid factors. I recomputed those factors in float64 and kept the original files and repair records. The data, shuffle and regularizer stayed fixed. All Rosetta maps passed the original check.

Independent checks reproduced the matching scores from saved states and verified the generated outputs, file identities and ratings.
